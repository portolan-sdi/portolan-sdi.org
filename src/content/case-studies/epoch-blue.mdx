## Introducing Epoch

[Epoch](https://epoch.blue/about) provides the data needed to make the first mile of supply chains visible to platforms, companies and suppliers. Its mission is to de-commoditise supply chains through first-mile data, supporting decisions on deforestation, legality, labour rights and carbon disclosure.

Epoch's open data programme focuses on opening up the fundamental datasets needed to meet the obligations of regulations like the EUDR. Until now, these datasets were locked behind commercial platforms or manual downloads from clunky websites. By publishing them in cloud-native formats with agent-ready discovery, anyone with a coding agent can start assessing the landscape around sourcing locations, and Epoch's own infrastructure benefits from the same open formats.

## Uncovering "ghost" forests with Portolan

One of our customers sources poplar plywood from eastern China. Their supplier declared its harvest area as 8 pins on a Google Earth screenshot, covering over 30,000 hectares. Its FSC certificate says the villages inside hold 6,500 hectares of poplar plantation, under a forest plan running from 2017 to 2026, and that certificate is audited every year. Going by the paperwork alone, you'd think you were in the clear!

We pointed an agent at the Forest Typology Portolan catalog, an open 10 m forest-type map we publish, and found no more than 70 hectares of trees in any year of the plan. Meta and WRI's 1 m canopy height map tells the same story: just 482 hectares of anything over 5 metres, mostly roadside and village trees. That's an order of magnitude short of what was certified, in a region where poplar is typically grown in distinct plantation grids.

The certificate made a claim, and open data let us check it. We went from a single supplier screenshot to a result we'd stand behind in hours: no supplier questionnaires and no self-reported answers. The baseline is public, so anyone can check our work.

## Looking forward

Epoch will continue to lead on making supply chain sourcing data open, and more accessible than before. Our next focus is on bringing in explicit commodity maps, particularly those mapping tropical perennials like cocoa, palm, coffee and rubber.

## Why Portolan

Publishing open data is only the first step; making it discoverable, usable and scalable is still not well defined. Portolan sets out best practices for building a catalog: cloud-native files, STAC metadata and an AGENTS.md, so humans and agents alike can use the data. Our explorer takes advantage of each of these to read from Source with no backend.
