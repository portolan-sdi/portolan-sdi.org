## Background

A river can have monitoring stations, water-quality samples, and infrastructure records spread across different organizations. [GeoConnex](https://internetofwater.org/geoconnex/) gives those organizations a shared way to identify the river and connect their data to it.

The Lincoln Institute of Land Policy's [Center for Geospatial Solutions](https://www.lincolninst.edu/centers-initiatives/center-geospatial-solutions/key-initiatives/) develops GeoConnex with USGS and the Internet of Water Coalition. Publishers keep responsibility for their source data. GeoConnex harvests their metadata and builds the links that make it discoverable across organizational boundaries.

## Challenge

Finding water data is more complicated than searching for a river's name. The [Internet of Water describes](https://internetofwater.org/blog/uncategorized/geoconnex-community-system-for-water-data-management-usgs) incompatible naming conventions and metadata spread across agencies. Even a name such as Colorado River can refer to [different rivers](https://internetofwater.org/blog/unlocking-the-full-potential-of-water-data-usgs-and-the-internet-of-water). An analyst needs to establish which feature a record describes before combining it with anything else.

GeoConnex addresses that problem with persistent identifiers and a knowledge graph. The next publishing problem is making those linked features convenient for people working with spatial tables and web maps. A graph relationship, a queryable table, and a map each serve a different task.

## Solution, results, and impact

### Publish the features as well as the links

The [GeoConnex scheduler](https://github.com/internetofwater/scheduler/blob/main/userCode/lib/stac_catalog.py) now registers its existing exports in a Portolan catalog. It describes the files where they already live in cloud storage, with collection metadata, provenance, checksums, and documentation.

[GeoParquet](https://geoparquet.org/) provides the spatial tables for analysis, and [PMTiles](https://docs.protomaps.com/pmtiles/) provides the tiles for web maps. A separate export retains the original linked-data records for rebuilding the graph. The shared river identifiers connect these different views of the data.

### One river, a concrete query

The published reference-gage table includes a river identifier in its `mainstem_uri` column. Filtering it for the [Willamette River](https://geoconnex.us/ref/mainstems/1426120) returned **718 reference-gage records** on 2026-11-18. The graphic below plots that query's actual geometries and identifies records at Portland, Salem, and Eugene. The records include historical sites and are not a count of operating stations.

The same snapshot contains **852,673 mainstem records**, **203,525 reference-gage records**, and **49,346 public-water-system records** across those three exports. The [root catalog](https://storage.googleapis.com/harvest-geoconnex-us/stac/catalog.json) links 38 top-level collections. [The measurements and source files](/img/case-studies/geoconnex-measurements.json) document these figures.
