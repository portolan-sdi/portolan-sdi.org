## Background

[CARTO](https://carto.com/) builds software for spatial analytics and visualization. Its platform helps people work with location data through maps, analytical workflows, and AI agents.

[CARTO SDI](https://carto.com/spatial-data-infrastructure/) applies that work to spatial data infrastructure. It builds commercial services around a Portolan catalog that stays in storage controlled by the publisher.

## The challenge

Data publishers need to keep authoritative data available while maintaining the systems that serve it. Traditional portals add databases, map services, and APIs to that responsibility. Users still need specialist knowledge to find and combine datasets.

CARTO wanted an approach that also gives organizations control over storage location, query engines, and approved AI models. Public, internal, and licensed data need operational controls without making the underlying publication dependent on one vendor.

## Why Portolan

Portolan defines the shared publication underneath those services. [STAC](https://stacspec.org/en/) metadata describes the assets. [GeoParquet](https://geoparquet.org/), [COG](https://cogeo.org/), and [PMTiles](https://docs.protomaps.com/pmtiles/) let clients read data directly from storage. `README.md` and `AGENTS.md` explain the same data to people and agents.

For CARTO, that provides a foundation it can extend while preserving the publisher's choice of tools.

> The published data remains a standard Portolan catalog in the publisher's storage.

— Javier de la Torre, [CARTO's introduction to Portolan and CARTO SDI](https://carto.com/blog/introducing-portolan-and-carto-sdi/), September 2, 2026.

## The solution

CARTO SDI adds three parts around the open catalog.

- **A catalog interface** for discovering and accessing public, private, and licensed datasets.
- **An AI interface** for asking questions and receiving maps, source references, and reproducible queries.
- **A publisher control plane** for access requests, governance, usage analytics, quality feedback, and monetization.

Those services are CARTO's product features. The Portolan specification defines the published catalog they build on.

## Results and impact

CARTO's OGC Connect Helsinki demonstration assessed a proposed data center location in Espoo using several publishers' catalogs. The assessment linked its findings to source data and repeatable queries, while identifying data that still needed preparation.

The demonstration used storage in Finland, DuckDB, and Mistral. It showed how publishers can retain separate catalogs while people ask questions across them.
